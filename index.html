Ilsang Ohn

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Statistics at Inha University. I study the theory of statistical inference and learning: how fast a procedure learns, whether it adapts to structure it does not know in advance, and what its uncertainty statements actually guarantee. My current work centers on

  • Bayesian nonparametrics and variational Bayes: posterior contraction, adaptation, and approximate posteriors that keep optimal rates;
  • Online Bayesian learning: sequential prediction with approximate posteriors and expert aggregation;
  • Distribution-free uncertainty quantification: conformal prediction and highest-density sets, including online settings under distribution shift;
  • Statistical learning theory: fast rates and localization, with applications to deep neural networks.

Before joining Inha, I was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Applied and Computational Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Notre Dame, working with Lizhen Lin. I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from Seoul National University under the supervision of Yongdai Kim, and a B.S. in Physics from Seoul National University.
